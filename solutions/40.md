### 1. Assumptions

- The game has a high variance component due to the pot doubling feature from rolling doubles, particularly from the fourth roll onward.
- The threat of rolling a total of 7 and crashing the round gains significance in later rolls.
- Players are assumed to act rationally, aiming to maximize their probability of winning.
- Differences in scores affect decisions significantly, especially in the later rounds (when $D < 0$ or $D > 0$ is critical if $F = 1$).
- Having a greater number of opponents who bank before you (i.e., $B$ is high) reduces the risk of not banking, as it limits the competition within the round.
- As $R$ decreases, the urgency to secure points increases, especially when $F = 1$.
- We approximate risks and rewards using a linear model with the assumption of further complexities and nonlinearities due to the game's randomness.

### 2. Unified Formula

Let:

$$
T = \left( \frac{P}{7} \right) + \left( 10 \cdot \text{max}(0, -D) \cdot F \right) + \left( 5 \cdot (R^{-1}) \cdot (1 - B/N) \right)
$$

### 3. Banking Decision Rule

**BANK if \( P \geq T \); otherwise CONTINUE.**

### 4. Meaning of Each Term

- **\( \frac{P}{7} \):** Represents the baseline risk of continuing; derived from the probability of rolling a 7 (crash condition) in a single roll.

- **\( 10 \cdot \text{max}(0, -D) \cdot F \):** If you are behind ($D < 0$) and it's the final round ($F = 1$), the urgency to overcome the deficit is high, hence the product of the negative score difference scaled by a factor of 10 to give a more pronounced incentive to bank when trailing.

- **\( 5 \cdot (R^{-1}) \cdot (1 - B/N) \):** This term decreases as more opponents bank in the same round (i.e., decreasing $B/N$ ratio) and as fewer rounds remain, encouraging banking earlier to secure the current pot.

### 5. Mathematical Derivation

- **Probability of Rolling a 7 (Crash Condition):** The probability of rolling a 7 is $\approx \frac{1}{6}$ since there are six outcomes (6,1), (1,6), (5,2), (2,5), (4,3), (3,4).

- **Score Consideration for Final Round:** If in the final round and with a score deficit, there needs to be a significant motivation to grasp any point, modeled by a linear urgency from being behind.

- **Banking Against Opponents:** The formula considers competitors' positions by adding a competitiveness factor relative to potential banking opponents.

### 7. Manual Calculation Procedure

1. **Assess the Current Pot ($P$):** Understand the round's potential reward.
2. **Calculate the Baseline Risk:** Divide $P$ by 7.
3. **Final Round Deficit Adjustment:** If the final round applies ($F=1$) and $D < 0$, factor in the urgent need to build a lead.
4. **Competitive Stance:** Measure the position relative to other players who've banked, adjusted by the round's inverse.
5. **Compare and Decide:** Use the banking decision rule to act.

### 8. Weaknesses and Edge Cases

- **High Variance Rounds:** The formula may underestimate situations where consecutive doubling may occur, leading to unconsidered high pots.
- **Risk Overestimation:** For risk-averse players, the linear risk model linked to rolling 7 may not capture their banking tendency.
- **Complex Opponent Dynamics:** The simplistic model for accounting competitors may misjudge in games where players cooperate or play aggressively.
- **Unforeseen Situations:** Unusual dice streaks (e.g., continuous doubles) are not fully integrated into the decision-making process.
